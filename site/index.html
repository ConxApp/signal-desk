How to read this

Broad spike Chatter is 3σ+ above this entity's own 45-day baseline and spread across many communities and many accounts. Breadth is what separates a real trend from one thread going viral.
One viral thread Same volume spike, but concentrated in one place from few accounts. Usually noise.
Ahead of the street Consumer-community chatter is running well ahead of investing-forum chatter. This is the whole thesis — when the finance subs catch up, the edge is gone.
Finance chatter only The investing forums are talking but consumers are not. A stock story, not a product story.
Not priced in Attention is elevated but price and volume are still flat.
Already moved The stock has run more than 15% in five days. You are probably late.
Selling out Unusual density of “sold out”, “restock”, “can't find” language. In consumer products this leads reported revenue more reliably than sentiment does.
positive negative The tone of the chatter, from sentiment plus the balance of buying language (“just copped”, “sold out”) against complaint language (“returned it”, “overrated”). Reported separately from the score, because a brand melting down is a loud, high-attention event that is not the same finding as a brand taking off. Negative names are deliberately pushed down the ranking.
chatter Robust z-score of mentions as a share of everything sampled that day. Median/MAD, so an old spike doesn't poison the baseline.
breadth 0–1. Distinct communities, distinct authors, and how evenly spread the mentions are.
lead Consumer chatter ÷ finance chatter. Above 2.5× means the street hasn't noticed.

This is a research tool, not advice. A social spike is a hypothesis about attention, not a forecast of revenue or price. Plenty of things trend hard and never sell, and plenty of good businesses never trend at all.

Scores below 21 days of history are unreliable by construction — the baseline isn't formed yet.
